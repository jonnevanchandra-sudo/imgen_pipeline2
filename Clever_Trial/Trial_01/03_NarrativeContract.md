# Narrative Contract
**Brand:** CLEVER Protein
**Campaign:** The 3 PM Office Rescue (3點鐘辦公室救星)
**Framework:** Narrative Framework v3
**Stage:** 3 — Emotional Interpretation

---

## Inputs Consumed

- StrategicDirection: "The Smart 3pm Swap"
- IdentityMigration: Office worker caught in the 3pm willpower battle → Office worker who has quietly 'solved' the 3pm problem (activation vector)
- ActivatedDimensions: Confidence (Critical), Accessibility (Critical), Performance (High)
- BehavioralFrame: The 3pm Snack Swap as a Solved Habit
- AudienceTakeaway: "Swapping my usual 3pm snack for CLEVER Weight Down is the obviously smarter move — same satisfaction, none of the hidden calories"

---

## NarrativeLensSelection

**Primary Lens: Calm Mastery**
How does competence feel when it becomes natural? → The 3pm decision used to be a daily contest between intention and temptation. For someone who has made the swap, it isn't a contest anymore — it's just what they do. Competence here isn't visible effort or discipline on display; it's the quiet absence of struggle where struggle used to be.

**Secondary Lens: Quiet Confidence**
How can confidence be expressed without dominance? → The viewer doesn't need to announce that they've figured something out. The confidence is internal — a small, private sense of "I've got this part handled" that doesn't require anyone else's validation or attention.

**Emotional Intensity: Medium**
Rationale: The campaign rests on a clear before/after contrast (the 3pm temptation vs. the smart swap), so the emotional progression must be legible at a glance — this rules out Low intensity's purely observational register. But the resolution must stay within CLEVER's calm, restrained tone — this rules out High intensity's elevated/cinematic register. Medium intensity delivers a readable emotional shift (temptation → quiet resolve) without dramatizing it into a transformation arc.

---

## CurrentEmotionalState

It's 3pm. The morning's focus has worn thin, and a low, restless hunger has crept in — not real hunger, more a craving for a break, a small treat, something to mark the shift into the afternoon's second half. This is the moment the day's earlier intentions feel most negotiable. Whatever happens next — whether it reinforces the day's goals or quietly chips away at them — happens almost automatically, on autopilot, without much deliberation.

There's a pull toward the familiar ritual: the snack run, the drink order, the shared "let's get something" moment with colleagues. It's comfortable. It's social. It doesn't feel like a decision at all.

---

## PsychologicalFriction

The barrier isn't a lack of knowledge — the viewer already knows, in general terms, that the 3pm snack run isn't doing their goals any favors. The barrier is the belief that choosing differently means choosing *less*: less satisfaction, less participation in the shared moment, more effort, more thinking about food than they want to at 3pm.

Choosing "the healthy thing" has historically felt like opting out — like doing something separate, deliberate, and slightly joyless while everyone else gets to enjoy the easy, automatic choice. That anticipated friction (extra effort, social separateness, reduced satisfaction) is often enough on its own to make the easier, less-smart option win by default.

---

## DesiredTransformation

**restless/tempted → settled/decided**
**anticipated friction → quiet ease**
**"I have to resist" → "I already chose"**

The shift isn't dramatic — it's the difference between a moment that used to require a small internal negotiation and a moment that no longer does. The viewer arrives at 3pm and, instead of weighing temptation against willpower, simply reaches for the thing that already fits. The relief isn't "I was strong enough to say no" — it's "there was nothing to resist in the first place."

---

## IdentityArrival

Someone for whom the 3pm moment has stopped being a small daily test. Not someone visibly disciplined or someone making a point of their choices — just someone who has quietly removed one recurring decision from their day. Competent in an understated way: the kind of competence that looks, from the outside, like nothing happened at all.

---

## ViewerTakeaway

*"Oh — that's just the easy swap. I could just do that."*

The residue isn't inspiration or aspiration toward a future self — it's the recognition of a small, low-stakes substitution that's easy to imagine making tomorrow. Not "I should be more disciplined," but "that's obviously the smarter pick, and it doesn't cost me anything."
