Narrative:

  Inputs  
  - Brand Contract: CLEVER Protein – “Refreshing performance for people who want results without the burden.” Clean, intelligent, Japanese-made clear WPI; lifestyle-accessible; quiet, evidence-backed confidence.  
  - Strategy Contract: Recode 3:30pm “guilty snacking” for Hong Kong office ladies into a smart, light-beauty hydration ritual using Japanese clear protein as an afternoon tea replacement.

  NarrativeLensSelection  
  - Primary lens: Aspirational Transformation – “Guilty snacker” → “Smart, light-beauty office lady.”  
  - Supporting lenses:  
    - Quiet Confidence – confidence expressed as calm, image-smart choice rather than discipline or sacrifice.  
    - Modern Freedom – removing friction and guilt from the 3:30pm moment.  
    - Emotional Recovery – releasing the emotional burden of “I shouldn’t have eaten that.”  
    - Effortless Capability – making the switch feel like a small, elegant hack, not a big lifestyle change.  
  - Emotional Intensity: Medium – clear emotional arc, visible but not dramatic transformation; grounded in everyday office reality.

  CurrentEmotionalState  
  - Baseline feelings at 3:30pm:  
    - Mentally drained from the workday; mild stress, low energy.  
    - Physically: a mix of hunger, sugar craving, and slight bloating from earlier meals.  
    - Emotionally:  
      - Low-level guilt and self-critique around snacks, calories, and body shape (“I know this isn’t good for my waistline/skin, but I’m tired.”).  
      - Subtle shame about “lack of discipline,” but not extreme—more like a quiet discomfort.  
      - Feeling that afternoon indulgence conflicts with the desired “light, polished office lady” self-image.  
    - Identity state:  
      - Sees herself as health-aware but not fully “doing it right.”  
      - Feels that her habits don’t quite match the slim, glowing, put-together version of herself she wants to project at work.  
    - Social/psychological context:  
      - Afternoon tea is a shared cultural habit; there’s comfort and belonging in snacking with colleagues.  
      - But there is also comparison and silent pressure to stay slim and “camera-ready” for social media and office appearances.

  PsychologicalFriction  
  - Key barriers between current and desired state:  
    - Category Misfit: Protein drinks are coded as “gym bro, heavy, milky, for workouts” – not as an elegant office 3:30pm drink.  
    - Sensory Mismatch: She expects protein to feel thick, powdery, and stomach-filling in a heavy way, conflicting with her desire for lightness and comfort in office clothes.  
    - Identity Risk: She doesn’t want to look like she’s “on a diet” or “doing bodybuilding” at her desk; that feels socially awkward and off-brand for her image.  
    - Guilt Attachment: The 3:30 snack is emotionally tied to “cheat” and “treat” – a small rebellion against constant self-control. Letting go of that feels like losing a comforting ritual.  
    - Effort Perception: Healthier options are perceived as complicated (tracking, planning, special diets) and mentally tiring—not something she wants to deal with mid-workday.  
    - Outcome Skepticism: “High protein” sounds useful but also sounds like “high calories” or “for muscle,” making her unsure it can be a smarter, lighter alternative to her usual snacks.  
  - Emotional summary of friction:  
    - Fear that changing the ritual means sacrifice, judgment, or standing out.  
    - Doubt that a protein drink could be both truly light and genuinely helpful for figure and skin.  
    - Attachment to the comfort and social belonging of current 3:30 habits.

  DesiredTransformation  
  - Emotional evolution targeted (medium intensity, everyday scale):  
    - From:  
      - Low-key guilt and self-critique after snacking.  
      - Confusion about how to eat “right” at work without effort.  
      - Quiet fear that every snack is working against her beauty and body goals.  
    - To:  
      - Calm, light satisfaction after 3:30pm—both physically and emotionally.  
      - Subtle pride in having made a “smart” choice that matches her self-image.  
      - A sense of alignment between what she wants (pleasure, satiety, social participation) and what she does (a clear, high-protein drink that supports her figure and skin).  
  - Transformation arc:  
    - Confusion & guilt → Clear understanding & reassurance (“this kind of protein is actually lighter than my usual snack”).  
    - Heavy, “cheat” feeling → Light, refreshing, beauty-support feeling.  
    - Quiet self-disappointment → Quiet self-respect and satisfaction.  
    - “I shouldn’t have had that” → “I’m glad I chose this; it fits who I want to be.”  
  - Emotional constraints:  
    - No dramatic before/after body stories; change is framed as a smart daily ritual, not a life overhaul.  
    - No fear escalation; tension stays at the level of familiar, manageable guilt and aspiration.  
    - Resolution is earned through simple, believable substitution—same 3:30 moment, different glass.

  IdentityArrival  
  - Who she becomes emotionally through this ritual:  
    - “Smart, light-beauty office lady”:  
      - She sees herself as someone who cares for her body and skin in a quiet, intelligent way.  
      - Her 3:30 choice reinforces her self-image instead of undermining it.  
    - Calmly confident:  
      - She doesn’t need to announce she’s being “healthy”; her choice quietly signals that she’s in control, not in struggle.  
      - She experiences competence as natural—this ritual feels integrated, not forced.  
    - Effortlessly modern:  
      - A refreshing, Japanese clear drink reads as stylish, contemporary, and aligned with East Asian “beauty from within” culture.  
      - She feels in sync with a subtle trend rather than doing something odd or extreme.  
    - Socially attuned, not isolated:  
      - She can still join the 3:30 break; her drink fits in visually and socially, while also slightly elevating her image.  
      - The ritual becomes a low-key status signal of being “health-smart” without being preachy.  
  - Identity meaning:  
    - “I am someone who manages my cravings and my image intelligently, in a way that feels light and kind to myself.”  
    - “My afternoon treat is part of my beauty routine, not something I have to mentally ‘undo’ later.”

  ViewerTakeaway  
  - Core emotional residue after viewing:  
    - “This feels like me, just a bit smarter and lighter.”  
    - “Swapping my 3:30 snack for this clear Japanese protein drink is an easy, guilt-free upgrade—not a restriction.”  
    - “Protein can actually be a refreshing, pretty afternoon drink that helps my figure and skin, not a heavy gym shake.”  
    - “I can start this tomorrow without changing my whole life—just change what’s in my cup.”  
  - Desired mental conclusions (lightweight, memorable):  
    - “3:30pm doesn’t have to mean guilt.”  
    - “A clear, fruity Japanese protein drink is the smarter version of the afternoon tea I already love.”  
    - “This is an attainable, office-friendly beauty habit I can own.”
