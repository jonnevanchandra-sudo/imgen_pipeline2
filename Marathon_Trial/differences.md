# Marathon Trial: v1 vs v2 Differences

## Overview
v1 and v2 are **two parallel creative explorations** of the Marathon Sports HK campaign, testing different emotional directions. Both share the same business objective (free fitting bookings) and core scenario, but diverge on emotional framing:

- **v1**: "Modern Freedom" — emphasizes simplification and clarity (constraint removal)
- **v2**: "Quiet Confidence" — emphasizes reassurance and human support

This explores how different emotional lenses create different *feelings* while preserving the same *mechanics* and scenario.

---

## 1. Campaign Contract Differences

### Theme Token
- **v1**: `FindYourFit` (single, fit-focused theme)
- **v2**: `Stop Guessing / Find Your Fit` (dual themes, adds the guessing/decision burden)

**Impact**: v2 acknowledges the problem state explicitly before the solution.

### Primary Message
- **v1** (longer): "Marathon Sports HK helps runners find the running shoe that truly fits their feet by comparing multiple brands under one roof with expert local fitting guidance."
- **v2** (more direct): "Casual runners should stop guessing and find the running shoe that truly fits them at Marathon Sports HK."

**Impact**: v2 speaks directly to the audience and problem, shorter and more conversational.

### Message Tokens Format
- **v1**: `CamelCase` tokens (e.g., `StopGuessing`, `FindYourFit`, `OneBrandFitsAll`)
- **v2**: `snake_case` tokens (e.g., `stop_guessing`, `find_your_fit`, `one_brand_fits_all`)

**Impact**: v2 uses consistent technical naming; no functional change but more standardized.

### New Required Copy in v2
- v2 adds: **"Try every brand, buy only yours"** (not present in v1)

**Impact**: This phrase reinforces the "personal fit" positioning vs. generic multi-brand availability.

### Overall Structure
- **v1**: More comprehensive, longer explanations in each section
- **v2**: More concise, streamlined for clarity

**Impact**: v2 is easier to parse and communicate downstream.

## 2. Strategy Contract Differences

### ActivatedDimensions
Both versions activate the same six dimensions (Confidence, Accessibility, Performance, Community, Belonging, Transformation), but the language shifts:

- **v1**: "recognizable brands, concrete offers, store availability, product names, shopping mechanics, and familiar local phrasing"
- **v2**: "helpful product benefits, community proof, and easy entry rather than distant luxury authority"

**Impact**: v2 emphasizes *human proof and relational trust* over operational clarity.

### ContextualTensions - NEW Tension in v2
**v2 adds a sixth tension not in v1:**
- **Current**: "Global running brands create excitement but also pressure to choose based on hype or loyalty."
- **Desired**: "Global brand breadth becomes useful because Marathon Sports HK acts as the local curator and fitting guide."
- **Type**: Operational

**Impact**: v2 recognizes that global brand excitement is *ambiguous* — it can paralyze OR empower, depending on curation. v1 treated it as pure opportunity.

### Required Meaning Tokens
v2 adds new secondary tokens:
- `try_every_brand_buy_only_yours`
- `Nike_ASICS_Hoka_and_more_as_range_examples` (more explicit brand usage)
- `stride_goals_Hong_Kong_running_context`
- `beginner_friendly_non_jargon_guidance`

**Impact**: v2 is more specific about what "multi-brand" means in practice.

### Forbidden Resolutions (v2 adds specificity)
v2 adds:
- `guaranteed_pain_free_running` (not in v1)
- `specific_shoe_model_recommendation_without_supplied_requirement` (more explicit constraint)

**Impact**: v2 tightens guardrails around medical claims and model-specific marketing.

### Brand Integrity Rules
**v1** phrase: "Keep Marathon Sports HK as the campaign owner"  
**v2** phrase: "**Preserve Marathon Sports HK / 馬拉松 as campaign owner, curator, and trusted fitting guide**"

**Impact**: v2 adds the explicit "curator" role—this is central to the brand's meaning.

## 3. Narrative Contract Differences

### MAJOR: Primary Narrative Lens Shift
**This is the most significant creative difference:**

#### v1 Primary Lens: "Modern Freedom"
- **Category**: Constraint Reduction
- **Role**: "Remove the burden of decoding every running-shoe option alone."
- **Implication**: Freedom comes from *simplifying choices* (constraint removal).

#### v2 Primary Lens: "Quiet Confidence"
- **Category**: Internal Confidence
- **Role**: "The runner does not become a superior athlete or expert. They become quietly reassured."
- **Implication**: Freedom comes from *supported decision-making* (reassurance, not just simplification).

**Impact**: This is a fundamental **emotional pivot**. 
- v1 says: "Too many choices → fewer choices = freedom"
- v2 says: "Uncertainty → supported clarity = calm confidence"

The difference: **v1 is about *de-cluttering*, v2 is about *reassurance*.**

### Supporting Lens Change
**v1 has three lenses:**
1. Modern Freedom
2. Quiet Confidence
3. Effortless Capability

**v2 has three lenses (different order and third lens):**
1. Quiet Confidence (was secondary in v1, now primary)
2. Effortless Capability (same as v1)
3. Human Connection (was implicit, now explicit as third lens)

**v2 Addition**: "Which relationship creates meaning? Staff expertise becomes emotionally important because the runner is not alone in the decision. The relationship is not 'expert above beginner,' but 'local guide beside runner.'"

**Impact**: v2 emphasizes the *relationship* between runner and staff. This is why images might look similar—same situation (fitting, shoes) but different *feeling* (alone vs. supported).

### Format Difference
- **v1**: JSON structure
- **v2**: YAML/Markdown structure with numbered sections

**Impact**: v2 is more human-readable and narrative-focused rather than data-structure-focused.

### Emotional Intensity
- **v1**: "Medium" (same as v2)
- **v2 rationale**: "The emotional volume should be clear but controlled. The story should have recognizable emotional movement without becoming overly cinematic, mythic, fear-based, or dramatic."

**Impact**: v2 provides explicit guardrails against melodrama.

### Current Emotional State Phrasing
**v1**: Lists emotional truth as bullet points in a compact section.  
**v2**: Expands this into a full paragraph: "They see too many brands, models, opinions, shoe technologies, and online recommendations. Nike, ASICS, Hoka, and other brands create excitement, but also make the decision feel heavier because each brand seems to ask for commitment."

**Impact**: v2 recognizes that brand excitement is *itself a source of anxiety*—a more nuanced understanding.

### Psychological Friction Framing
**v1 primary friction**: "Choice overload plus purchase-risk anxiety"  
**v2 primary friction**: "Decision burden" with explicit sub-note: "I don't know enough to choose well."

**Impact**: v2 reframes from external factors (too many choices, expensive cost) to internal confidence gap (knowledge inadequacy).

### Desired Transformation: v2 Adds Negative Resolutions
v2 explicitly states what the resolution is **NOT**:
- "Buy this model."
- "Become a serious runner."
- "This shoe prevents injury."
- "This brand is best."

v1 does NOT have these explicit negatives.

**Impact**: v2 is more rigorous about preventing scope creep into undesired territory.

### Identity Arrival Difference
**v1**: "Confident everyday Hong Kong runner"  
**v2**: "Confident fit-led runner" with explicit note that "They are not necessarily faster, more advanced, or more technical. They are someone who feels more capable because the decision has become understandable."

**Impact**: v2 explicitly guards against the "transformation into elite athlete" trap.

### Narrative Boundaries Section
**v1**: Lists what narrative "must not define" (visual concept, scene layout, camera, etc.)  
**v2**: Adds explicit section "Must Preserve" and "Must Avoid" with specific examples of each.

**Impact**: v2 is more prescriptive about downstream guardrails.

### Compressed Narrative Thesis
**v1**: Thesis embedded throughout sections  
**v2 provides explicit final thesis**: "Marathon Sports HK helps unsure Hong Kong runners stop guessing and start choosing with confidence — not by pushing one brand, but by making fit feel clear, personal, local, and easy to act on."

**Impact**: v2 ends with a clear, concise thesis that unifies all sections.

## Why Images Look Similar (But Feel Different)

The visual *content* remains the same across v1 and v2:
- Runners in store
- Multi-brand shoes visible
- Staff/expert guidance
- Shopping/fitting scenario

**Why images look similar:**
The core *scene* (what happens) is identical. Both campaigns show fitting/choice/guidance.

**Why images feel different (and should differ downstream):**

1. **v1 (Modern Freedom)** emphasizes: *Simplification, clarity, choice abundance reduced to manageable*
   - Visual tone: Clean, uncluttered, options visible but organized
   - Lighting: Bright, clear, minimal confusion
   - Composition: Order emerging from chaos

2. **v2 (Quiet Confidence)** emphasizes: *Reassurance, human support, calm decision-making*
   - Visual tone: Warm, relational, human connection visible
   - Lighting: Warm, approachable, human faces/expressions matter
   - Composition: The guide-beside-runner relationship visible

**The art direction downstream should reflect this:** v2's Composition & Rendering layer should prioritize *human connection and warmth* over *organizational clarity*. Both show shoes, but v2 puts more emphasis on the **staff member's reassuring posture/expression** and the **runner's calm engagement**.

## Conclusion

**v1 and v2 are equal-weight creative explorations, not an evolution.**

Shared foundation (both campaigns):
- Target casual/beginner Hong Kong runners
- Drive free fitting bookings
- Emphasize multi-brand choice under one roof
- Avoid medical claims and single-brand pushing

**The fork:**
- **v1 (Modern Freedom)**: Focus on *making choice easier through simplification and clarity* — organizational, clean, order-from-chaos
- **v2 (Quiet Confidence)**: Focus on *making the runner feel supported through human connection* — relational, warm, guidance-beside-you

This is why downstream execution differs. v1's Composition & Rendering emphasizes balanced clarity (four equally bright elements). v2's emphasizes human warmth (two bright human subjects, products supporting them).

**Why images look similar:** The *situation* is identical. But the *emotional direction* splits into two valid paths: "freedom through simplification" (v1) vs "confidence through support" (v2). This difference should emerge most clearly in the Composition & Rendering stage (6).

# Pipeline Reflection: Do v1 and v2 Directions Show Up Downstream?

**Answer: YES. v1 and v2 diverge clearly at stages 4 & 6 (execution layers). Stage 5 (scene setup) stays neutral.**

## Stage 4: Art Direction — Conceptual Divergence

**v1 Direction: Modern Freedom**
- VisualConcept: "The Guided Fit Checkpoint"
- Metaphor: transition/clarity pathway
- Core: "uncertainty has a clear next step"
- Primary Relationship: "Supported self-direction" (shopper agency emphasis)

**v2 Direction: Quiet Confidence**
- VisualConcept: "Guided Choice Made Clear"
- Metaphor: human mentor relationship
- Concept types: "Mentor Relationship," "Lifestyle Integration"
- Core: "this feels easy enough to try"
- Primary Relationship: "Local guide beside runner" (human warmth emphasis)

**v2 includes StaffRunnerDynamic section** with explicit sub-sections (ExpertiseRole, RunnerRole, DecisionOwnership) that v1 doesn't detail. This reinforces the relational focus.

**Verdict**: Stage 4 shows the two directions clearly diverge in concept and relationship framing.

---

## Stage 5: Scene Assembly ⚠️ MINIMAL SHIFT

Both versions describe the same scene (runner + staff + shoes + store). 

Minor language tweaks:
- **v1**: "fitting checkpoint"
- **v2**: "fitting moment where uncertainty becomes supported comparison"

**v2 coherence_rules** add: "collaborative, friendly, low-pressure fitting guidance" (v1 doesn't explicitly say this)

**Verdict**: Same scene, slightly warmer emotional language. Not a visual change.

## Stage 6: Composition & Rendering — Visual Execution Divergence

**This is where the emotional directions become visual decisions.**

### HierarchyPlan Comparison

**v1 (Modern Freedom direction):**
```
PRIMARY (all 4 elements equally bright):
  - Runner
  - Expert
  - Shoes
  - Fitting station
```
*Strategy: Balanced retail demonstration; all pieces of the service equally visible.*

**v2 (Quiet Confidence direction):**
```
PRIMARY (only 2 elements, brightest):
  - Runner
  - Expert

SECONDARY (3 elements, dimmer):
  - Fitting station
  - Shoes
  - Marathon Sports HK branding
```
*Strategy: Prioritize human relationship; products support the moment.*

**Impact**: Different hierarchy reflects different emotional priorities. v1 emphasizes all elements of the service equally. v2 emphasizes the human connection as primary.

### CameraPlan: Depth of Field Strategy

**v1 (Modern Freedom direction)**: f/5.6 "Atmospheric" depth of field
- Softly blurs background
- Isolates the central interaction
- Reasoning: Background softens so it "does not compete with the primary service cluster"

**v2 (Quiet Confidence direction)**: f/8 "Environmental" depth of field
- Keeps background sharper, maintains context
- Reasoning: "The background carries meaningful communication... multi-brand range, brand ownership, Hong Kong relevance..."

**Different choices, different meaning:**
- v1 isolates the service moment from context
- v2 contextualizes the moment within the Marathon Sports HK environment

### LightingPlan: Emotional Tone in Light

**v1**: (not explicitly specified in contract; implied: functional/clarity-focused)

**v2 explicitly specifies**:
- "soft_retail_key_light_on_runner_and_staff"
- "subtle_rim_lighting_emphasis"
- "controlled_overhead_fill"

**Directional difference**: v1 implies functional clarity lighting. v2 explicitly designs for warmth and human connection.

### AttentionPlan — Eye Flow

**v1**: 9-step progression through many elements
**v2**: 6-step, streamlined progression focused on human subjects

**v2 is tighter, relationship-focused.**

**Verdict**: Stage 6 execution shows major visual differences between the two directions. v1 prioritizes balanced demonstration of all service elements. v2 prioritizes human warmth and relational support.
