# Narrative Contract
**Brand:** Nike
**Campaign:** Nike Chill Run Club
**Framework:** Narrative Framework v3
**Stage:** 3 — Emotional Interpretation

---

## Inputs Consumed

- StrategicDirection: "The city at night is yours to claim — if you run through it with someone else who chose to show up."
- IdentityMigration: Drained Office Worker → Night City Protagonist (activation vector)
- ActivatedDimensions: Community Belonging (Critical), Individual Transformation (High), Accessibility (High), Lifestyle-Integrated Sport (Supporting)
- BehavioralPositioning: After-work run as reclaiming personal identity through community
- AudienceTakeaway: "Running after work with someone I actually like — this is what my evenings could look like."

---

## NarrativeLensSelection

**Primary Lens: Social Liberation**
What does the run unlock? → Not just physical movement — the permission to be a person again after a day that required you to be a professional. The run unlocks this because you're doing it WITH someone. Showing up for a run is an act of agency, but showing up WITH someone is an act of belonging. The liberation is shared — which is what makes it real and repeatable, not just a one-off escape.

**Secondary Lens: Main Character Moment**
The brief is explicit: *做自己嘅主角* — be the main character of your own life. The HK night is cinematic. City lights, harbor water, warm amber streetlamps. Running through that at 8pm after work, with someone beside you who also chose this, is a genuinely protagonist-level moment. The city is yours tonight. The image should make the viewer feel that.

**Emotional Intensity: Medium**
Instagram lifestyle format, Gen Z HK audience. The emotional register must be warm and immediately legible — aspirational but real, not cinematic or elevated. The viewer must feel "I know exactly what that feels like" within two seconds. The image earns attention through social recognition, not emotional spectacle.

---

## CurrentEmotionalState

The viewer arrives at the end of a Hong Kong workday. Not just physically depleted — identity-depleted. Eight or ten hours of performing professionalism: responsive, presentable, productive. By evening they have compressed themselves into the requirements of the job. They believe rest means stillness. Dinner at home, phone, sleep. Their social life is something they intend to have when they are less busy.

The city outside is alive at this hour — city lights on, harbor glowing, people moving through it. But the commute home just treats it as a tunnel. The city belongs to everyone else tonight.

---

## PsychologicalFriction

The combined barrier is a habit reinforced by exhaustion: *I don't have anything left to give socially or physically after this day.*

Running carries an additional layer: it signals athletic identity that most HK young professionals do not claim. "Running club" sounds like something for committed athletes, not tired office workers who haven't run since secondary school. The social barrier compounds the physical one — joining a group means being seen, being measured, potentially being the worst one there.

The friction is: *I'm too tired, and I'm not the kind of person who does things like this.*

---

## DesiredTransformation

**exhaustion → presence**
**isolation → social warmth**
**work identity → personal identity**

The transformation doesn't happen at a finish line. It happens mid-run — in a specific kind of social moment: a shared laugh at something one of them says, or the comfortable silence of two people whose breathing has synchronized, or the glance that passes between two people who both know they made the right call tonight.

In that moment, the person realizes the workday is gone. They are not thinking about their inbox. The city is beautiful. They are genuinely here, with someone they like, doing something that belongs entirely to them.

The run didn't require them to be athletic. It required them to show up. The other person did the rest.

---

## IdentityArrival

Someone who has reclaimed their evenings. Someone whose post-work life is real and happening — not pending. Someone who knows their city at night, not just through a commute window. Someone who has a social life that exists outside the office, anchored by something as simple as a weekly run with the right person. Someone who is the main character of their own Tuesday evening.

---

## ViewerTakeaway

*"That's what my city looks like at night when you actually go outside. And they look like they're genuinely having a good time — not performing it, not doing it for the content, just actually enjoying it. Running together at night in HK looks like that. I could do that. We could do that."*

The residue is possibility — specifically social possibility. Not the possibility of becoming a better athlete. The possibility of an evening that looks and feels like theirs.

---

## Narrative Requirements

The image must show genuine social energy between subjects — not implied warmth, not synchronized stride as a metaphor for connection. Real social interaction: a shared laugh, a glance that communicates something between the two people, the comfortable proximity of two people who are actually friends doing something they chose to do together. This is what the brief means by Chill Run Club. The social moment is the product.

The HK night environment is not a backdrop — it is part of what the subjects are experiencing. The city at night is something they are moving through, and the image should communicate that this city, at this hour, belongs to people who choose to be in it.
