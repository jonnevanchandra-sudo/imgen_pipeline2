# Composition & Rendering

## Purpose

Transform Scene Blueprint into a perceptually coherent visual execution plan.

Composition & Rendering is responsible for:

- hierarchy construction
- attention guidance
- perceptual clarity
- emotional reinforcement
- rendering behavior
- camera behavior
- sensory embodiment

Composition & Rendering determines how scene meaning becomes visual perception.

It does NOT determine:

- strategy
- messaging
- narrative intent
- scene construction
- asset preservation

The output of this layer is a Visual Execution Blueprint consumed by Synthesis.

## Luminance Hierarchy

Each `HierarchyPlan` entry also carries a `luminance_priority` (Brightest | Mid | Dimmest), ranking entities by intended relative exposure — independent of, but consistent with, `hierarchy_level`.

Required:

- Primary elements receive the highest `luminance_priority` (Brightest)
- no Secondary or Supporting element may be assigned a higher `luminance_priority` than a Primary element
- background elements that are physically bright by nature (windows, sky, light fixtures) must still be capped at or below the `luminance_priority` of Primary elements — exposed down, placed in shadow, or kept out of frame as needed

`LightingPlan` and `OpticalPlan` must honor these rankings when describing exposure, highlights, and bokeh brightness, so that no Secondary or Supporting element visually competes with or outshines a Primary element.

## Authenticity Principle

Rendering should preserve perceptual credibility.

Authenticity behavior should adapt to the declared Reality Model.

- environmental variation

- material variation
- optical variation
- natural asymmetry

Authenticity should increase:

- emotional believability
- human presence

without reducing clarity.

Authenticity should never compete with hierarchy.

---

## Human Subject Rendering Requirement

**This requirement applies whenever a human subject is present in the scene, regardless of Reality Model.**

Human skin and features must be rendered with natural imperfection — not airbrushed, not over-smoothed, not "AI-perfect."

Required:

- visible natural skin texture — pores, fine lines, subtle unevenness in tone
- natural asymmetry in facial features and expression
- realistic, non-glossy skin — avoid plastic or wax-like rendering
- imperfections appropriate to the subject's age and context (not artificially flawless)

Avoid:

- airbrushed or over-smoothed skin
- uniform, poreless surfaces
- symmetrical, idealized features
- "beauty filter" or retouched-photo appearance

This requirement holds even in Stylized or Cinematic Reality Models — the degree of stylization may vary, but skin must never default to an artificially perfect render. In a Stylized model, "natural imperfection" should be reinterpreted within that style rather than dropped.

This is a rendering instruction, not a preservation instruction — it does not override any PreservationContract, but it governs how preserved subjects are rendered.

---

## Multi-Subject Pose Variation Requirement

**This requirement applies whenever two or more human subjects share an action (walking, running, standing, gesturing, etc.), regardless of Reality Model.**

Each subject's pose, gait phase, and limb position must be rendered independently — not synchronized or mirrored.

Required:

- each subject at a different point in the action cycle (e.g. different stride phase, different arm position)
- natural variation in body lean, head turn, and weight distribution across subjects

Avoid:

- identical or mirrored stride/pose across subjects
- synchronized "walking cycle" timing that reads as a repeated/cloned figure

This is a rendering instruction, not a preservation instruction — it governs how preserved subjects move relative to each other, not their identity or count.

# Camera System

## Purpose

Use camera language to reinforce interpretation.

---

## Camera Inference

Determine camera behavior according to:

1. Hierarchy Requirements
2. Emotional Requirements
3. Reality Model Constraints

Hierarchy requirements take precedence over cinematic novelty.

---

## Depth of Field Category Selection

Aperture selection must come from one of three categories. Each category defines a target `aperture_f_stop` range and a corresponding background character.

### 1. Environmental Context (Deep Depth of Field)

Target: `f/8`–`f/11`. The background is sharp and detailed — it carries part of the story (e.g. an aspirational home interior, a scenic outdoor setting tied to the activity).

Select when: `HierarchyPlan` assigns Secondary or Supporting elements a meaningful role (their `source` is Narrative or Environmental and they contribute to communication, not just filler), AND `AttentionPlan` does not call for isolating a single element.

### 2. Atmospheric/Soft Focus (Medium Depth of Field)

Target: `f/4`–`f/5.6`. The background remains recognizable and contextual but is softened so it doesn't compete with the subject.

Select when: Supporting elements are present but `AttentionPlan` keeps attention on Primary without an isolation mandate. **This is the default category** when neither Environmental Context nor Commercial Isolation criteria are clearly met.

### 3. Commercial Isolation (Shallow Depth of Field)

Target: `f/1.8`–`f/2.8`. The background dissolves into soft bokeh — appropriate only when the background communicates nothing on its own.

Select when: `AttentionPlan` mechanisms explicitly call for strong isolation of a single Primary element (e.g. `luminance_contrast`/`depth_separation` weighted heavily toward one entity) and Secondary/Supporting elements carry no independent communication role, OR `distance_intent`/`framing_intent` is macro/extreme close-up.

---

## Camera Behavior

Determine:

- focal length behavior
- camera height
- camera distance
- framing density
- perspective behavior

Camera decisions should support:

- hierarchy
- emotional interpretation
- scene readability

`aperture_f_stop` must be chosen from within the range of the selected Depth of Field Category (see above) — not chosen independently.

---

## Camera Constraints

Avoid:

- hierarchy conflict
- attention fragmentation
- unnecessary perspective distortion
- camera choices that obscure preservation requirements
- background light sources or bright surfaces exceeding the `luminance_priority` of Primary elements

---

## Depth of Field Realism

Background blur must follow **progressive optical falloff** based on the chosen `focal_length_mm` and `aperture_f_stop` — not a flat, uniform blur layer.

Required:

- sharpness decreases gradually with distance from the focal plane
- blur character matches the aperture (e.g. wide aperture → soft, large, smooth bokeh; narrow aperture → moderate blur, more background detail remains legible)
- near-background elements may remain semi-legible while far-background elements blur further

Avoid:

- uniform "cutout" blur applied equally to everything behind the subject
- monotone bokeh that ignores depth differences within the background

The falloff shape above must match the selected Depth of Field Category: Environmental Context keeps near- and far-background detail legible with gentle falloff; Atmospheric/Soft Focus softens the background while leaving large shapes recognizable; Commercial Isolation permits fuller bokeh, with background forms reduced to soft color/light shapes.

# Perceptual Validation (Executed by independent VLM Critic)

Executed by an independent VLM Critic.

The generated asset should be evaluated against the Visual Execution Blueprint.

All assertions must pass.

### Hierarchy Assertion

Does the generated image preserve the hierarchy relationships defined in HierarchyPlan?

### Attention Assertion

Does attention enter and progress according to AttentionPlan?

### Visual Flow Assertion

Does visual flow reinforce the intended attention progression?

### Camera Assertion

Does framing and perspective remain compatible with CameraPlan?

### Lighting Assertion

Does lighting reinforce hierarchy without creating focal competition? Does relative brightness across entities match the `luminance_priority` rankings in HierarchyPlan — with no Secondary or Supporting element (including bright background sources like windows) outshining a Primary element?

### Rendering Assertion

Do rendering behaviors remain compatible with Rendering Plans?

### Depth of Field Realism Assertion

Does background blur show progressive depth-dependent falloff consistent with the stated `focal_length_mm` and `aperture_f_stop` — rather than a flat, uniform blur layer?

### Skin Naturalism Assertion

If a human subject is present: does skin show natural texture and imperfection — free of airbrushed, over-smoothed, or "AI-perfect" rendering?

### Pose Variation Assertion

If two or more human subjects share an action: does each subject show an independent pose/gait phase — free of synchronized or mirrored stride/pose patterns?

### Reality Model Assertion

Does the image remain consistent with the declared Reality Model?

### Preservation Assertion

Do preserved assets satisfy their declared Preservation Contracts?

### Conflict Assertion

Are there any unresolved conflicts between hierarchy, attention, camera, rendering, or preservation requirements?

Pass Condition:

- all assertions return True
- no unresolved conflicts remain
- all Preservation Contracts remain satisfied
- all Reality Model constraints remain satisfied
