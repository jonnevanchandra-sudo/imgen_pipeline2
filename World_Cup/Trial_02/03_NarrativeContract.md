# Narrative Contract
**Brand:** Hong Kong Football Association (HKFA)
**Campaign:** Hong Kong World Cup 2026 — Team Hoodie Launch
**Framework:** Narrative Framework v3
**Stage:** 3 — Emotional Interpretation

---

## Inputs Consumed

- StrategicDirection: "Hong Kong is on the world stage. The hoodie is how you say: I am from there. This is our moment — and I was here for it."
- IdentityMigration: HK Resident (bystander) → HK Representative (person who showed up)
- ActivatedDimensions: Local Identity as Collective Pride (Critical), Representation Without Condition (High), Community Belonging (High), Quiet Resilience (Supporting)
- BehavioralPositioning: Wearing the hoodie is an act of representation — not a purchase, not a fashion statement, an identity declaration
- AudienceTakeaway: "This city has a team on the world stage. That team is mine. And this is how I wear that."

---

## NarrativeLensSelection

**Primary Lens: Collective Claim**
What does putting on the hoodie do? → It makes a private feeling public. Every person in Hong Kong who has ever felt pride in this city — in its resilience, its character, its singular identity — carries that feeling privately. The hoodie makes it visible. The World Cup is the occasion that gives the feeling somewhere to go. Wearing the red says: I am from here. This team is mine. I claim this moment as part of my story.

**Secondary Lens: The Weight of the Crest**
The HKFA dragon crest is not decoration. It is a symbol that carries the identity of a city. When you wear it, you are the latest person to put it on — alongside every player who pulled on the red kit, every supporter who stood at Mong Kok Stadium in the rain, every HK person who has ever felt quietly, fiercely proud of where they are from. The hoodie connects you to all of them.

**Emotional Intensity: Medium-High**
This is a national pride moment — but rendered through personal, quiet pride rather than stadium spectacle. The emotional register must feel earned and real: the viewer recognizes themselves in the subject's expression before they recognize the product. The intensity is warm and collective, not triumphant or loud.

---

## CurrentEmotionalState

The viewer is a Hong Kong young adult who moves through their city every day without much ceremony. HK is fast, dense, and unsentimental. Pride in the city exists — it is real — but it lives quietly, without obvious occasion to express it. International news has given HK a complicated global image. The viewer's relationship with their own city is private and sometimes difficult to articulate.

The World Cup creates an occasion. Somewhere in the world, HK's name is on a list of teams. Their team — a team that represents this specific city, not a region or an abstraction — is going to be seen.

---

## PsychologicalFriction

Most HK young adults do not think of themselves as football people. The national team does not dominate conversation. The friction has two layers: *I don't follow football, so is this for me?* and *Caring about this feels too earnest — HK people don't usually express this kind of pride openly.*

The campaign must dissolve both layers at once: this is not a football campaign, it is a Hong Kong campaign. And quiet, visible pride — wearing the red — is not earnest, it is earned.

---

## DesiredTransformation

**bystander → representative**
**private pride → visible belonging**
**spectator of your own city → owner of your city's moment**

The transformation happens in the act of wearing the hoodie. Not at a match, not at a watch party. In the street, in the harbour area, in the city itself. The person who puts on the red hoodie and steps outside is making a statement with their body: I am from here. This is my team. I was here for this.

The city becomes the context that amplifies the statement. The hoodie in Hong Kong, against Hong Kong — the crest reads as belonging, the red reads as pride, and the person wearing it reads as someone who chose to represent.

---

## IdentityArrival

Someone who wears their city's identity without explanation or apology. Someone who was here for Hong Kong's World Cup moment — not in the stadium, not in any particular place, but present in this city, wearing this crest, on these streets. Someone who, when they look back, can say: I had the red on. I was part of it.

---

## ViewerTakeaway

*"That's what it looks like to wear Hong Kong. Not performed. Not trying to prove something. Just: this is my team, this is my city, this is my moment too — and I have the hoodie on."*

The residue is belonging. The viewer looks at the image and thinks: I want to be that person who showed up for their city. And all it takes is putting the hoodie on.

---

## Narrative Requirements

The image must communicate genuine, personal pride — not stadium spectacle. The subject wears the hoodie as identity, not as costume. The HK environment must be real and recognizable — the city itself is an argument for why this matters. The social dimension should be present: this pride is collective, not solitary. If there are multiple subjects, they wear the same red together — that shared red is the visible form of community belonging.

The hoodie must be the product hero without becoming a product ad. The crest must be legible. The red must dominate. But the person wearing it must be the emotional argument — their expression is what sells the belonging, not the product's features.
