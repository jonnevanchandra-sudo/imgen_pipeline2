# Art Direction Contract
**Brand:** Hong Kong Football Association (HKFA)
**Campaign:** Hong Kong World Cup 2026 — Team Hoodie Launch
**Framework:** Art Direction Framework v3
**Stage:** 4 — Visual Interpretation

---

## Inputs

**From Strategy Contract:**
- StrategicDirection: "Hong Kong is on the world stage. The hoodie is how you say: I am from there. This is our moment — and I was here for it."
- IdentityMigration: HK Resident → HK Representative (the act of wearing the hoodie makes the identity visible)
- ActivatedDimensions: Local Identity as Collective Pride (Critical), Representation Without Condition (High), Community Belonging (High)

**From Narrative Contract:**
- Primary Lens: Collective Claim — wearing the hoodie makes private pride public
- Secondary Lens: The Weight of the Crest — the dragon crest connects the wearer to every person who has ever worn HK's red
- DesiredTransformation: bystander → representative; private pride → visible belonging
- ViewerTakeaway: "That's what it looks like to wear Hong Kong. Not performed. Just: this is my city, this is my moment."
- Narrative Requirement: genuine personal pride, hoodie as product hero through the person wearing it, HK environment as identity argument

**Brand Guardrails (from BrandContract):**
- Sacred assets: HKFA dragon crest, red + white color system, "香港 HONG KONG" text, HK urban environment
- Protected truth: Emotional Pride > Performance; Community Belonging; Authenticity > Spectacle; Kit as Cultural Symbol
- Forbidden: Elite athlete framing, performance/technical sportswear positioning, competitive achievement language

## VisualIntent

**Wearing Hong Kong**

One or two HK young people standing in their city — wearing the red. The image is not about running, not about playing, not about winning. It is about presence. The person is here, in Hong Kong, with the dragon crest on their chest. The city behind them is recognizably HK. The image says: this is what it looks like when a Hong Kong person wears their team's identity — not performing it, not dressing up for it, just wearing it as naturally as they would wear anything that belongs to them.

---

## VisualConcept

**紅在香港 — Red in Hong Kong**

A Hong Kong young adult (or two) wearing the HK World Cup team hoodie, standing or walking in a recognizable HK urban environment — harbour area, urban street, city backdrop. The red of the hoodie reads against HK. The dragon crest is visible. The expression is not triumphant — it is the quiet confidence of someone who knows where they are from and is not uncomfortable saying so.

The visual headline is the red hoodie in the city. The emotional headline is the face of the person wearing it — present, grounded, proud without needing to announce it.

The image should feel documentary: this is a real person in their city, wearing their team's colors. Not a campaign. Not a production. Just: Hong Kong, here.

---

## SubjectRelationshipLogic

**Grounded Presence**

The subject's relationship with the city and with the camera is one of ownership — not in an aggressive sense, but in the sense of: *I belong here, and this belongs to me.* The HK environment is not a backdrop they have been placed in front of. It is theirs. The hoodie connects them to it — the red is the flag color, the crest is the city's symbol, and the person wearing it is the city's person.

If two subjects are present, they wear the red together. Their relationship communicates collective belonging: two people from the same city, claiming the same moment. No performance between them required — they simply share the same red, in the same city, at the same time.

The viewer's relationship to the image is recognition and belonging — *that could be me, that IS me, this is us.* Not aspiration toward someone unattainable. Identification with someone real.

## AttentionControl

**Focal Priority:** The subject's face — specifically the expression of quiet, personal pride — must be the first emotional read. The red hoodie and dragon crest read second, as the visual product of that pride. The HK environment reads third, as the place that makes the pride meaningful — *this is the city they are representing.*

**Color Dominance:** Red is the structural anchor of the image. The HK team red must dominate the frame through the hoodie — the subject's body carries the campaign's color argument. The city provides depth, texture, and identity; the red provides the visual punch.

**Product Legibility:** The HKFA dragon crest must be readable — the specific identity mark that makes the red hoodie the HK hoodie and not any red hoodie. This legibility is non-negotiable.

The image earns its second look: the first look is the person and the red; the second look is where you see the crest and recognize the campaign.

---

## DistinctivenessValidation

**Evaluated against:**
- Generic football team merchandise photography: studio product shot, white background, jersey isolated
- Sports brand campaign imagery: athletic body, performance posture, competition context
- Lifestyle streetwear content: product-hero photography, fashion mood, no emotional argument
- National pride stock photography: flags, spectators, stadium emotion

**Differentiation:**
This image must not read as a football ad, a merchandise photo, or a generic national pride visual. It must read as a portrait of a Hong Kong person wearing something that belongs to them — quiet, specific, real. The city context is what makes it local and honest. The face is what makes it personal. The crest is what makes it cultural.

**Anti-patterns confirmed avoided:**
- No stadium or match context — this is a street-level identity campaign, not a match-day campaign
- No athlete posing or performance posture
- No product-isolated photography — the hoodie is worn, by a real person, in a real place
- No triumphant or celebratory pose — quiet pride, not spectacle
- No tourist-postcard HK (no Victoria Peak overlook, no golden hour generic skyline) — real HK urban texture
